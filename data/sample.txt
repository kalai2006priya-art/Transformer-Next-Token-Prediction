The Fox and the Grapes.
A hungry Fox saw some bunches of ripe grapes hanging from a vine. He tried to jump up and reach them, but they were too high. After trying again and again without success, he turned away and said to himself, They are probably sour anyway, and I do not want them.

The Tortoise and the Hare.
A Hare was making fun of the Tortoise for being slow. Do you ever get anywhere? asked the Hare with a laugh. Yes, replied the Tortoise, and I get there sooner than you think. Let us run a race and see. The Hare agreed, confident of winning. When the race began, the Hare ran ahead and decided to take a nap, believing he had plenty of time. Meanwhile, the Tortoise kept walking slowly and steadily, never stopping. When the Hare woke up, he saw the Tortoise crossing the finish line. Slow and steady wins the race.

The Lion and the Mouse.
A little Mouse accidentally woke a sleeping Lion. The Lion placed his huge paw over the Mouse and prepared to eat him. Please spare my life, cried the Mouse, and one day I will repay your kindness. The Lion laughed at the idea of a small mouse helping a mighty lion, but he let the Mouse go. A few days later, the Lion was caught in a hunter's net. Hearing his roar, the Mouse ran to the spot and chewed through the ropes with his sharp teeth. The Lion was set free, realizing that even the smallest friend can be of great help.

The Ant and the Grasshopper.
During the bright summer days, an Ant worked tirelessly, collecting grains of wheat and storing them for the coming winter. A Grasshopper was singing and enjoying the sunshine, mocking the Ant for working so hard. Why worry about winter now? asked the Grasshopper. When winter arrived, cold winds blew and snow covered the fields. The Grasshopper had no food and was starving, while the Ant and his family lived comfortably on the food they had gathered. It is wise to prepare today for the needs of tomorrow.

The Boy Who Cried Wolf.
A young shepherd boy watched over a flock of sheep near a village. To amuse himself, he ran toward the village shouting, Wolf! Wolf! The villagers rushed out to help him, only to find no wolf. The boy laughed at the trick. A few days later, he played the same trick again, and once more the villagers came running. Later, a real wolf emerged from the forest and attacked the sheep. The boy cried for help with all his might, but no one came, thinking he was lying again. Nobody believes a liar, even when he tells the truth.

The Wind and the Sun.
The Wind and the Sun argued about which of them was stronger. They saw a traveler walking along the road wearing a warm cloak. Let us agree, said the Sun, that whoever can make the traveler take off his cloak is the stronger one. The Wind tried first. He blew a fierce and freezing blast, but the harder he blew, the tighter the traveler wrapped his cloak around himself. Then the Sun shone with gentle warmth. Soon the traveler felt the heat, unbuttoned his cloak, and finally took it off to rest in the shade. Gentleness and persuasion succeed where force fails.

The Crow and the Pitcher.
A thirsty Crow found a pitcher with a little water at the bottom. His beak could not reach the water, no matter how hard he tried. He looked around and saw small pebbles on the ground. One by one, he picked up the pebbles and dropped them into the pitcher. With each pebble, the water level rose higher. At last, the water reached the brim, and the clever Crow drank his fill. Little by little does the trick.
